1. Title: Language Models as Zero-Shot Planners: Extracting Actionable Knowledge for Embodied Agents

2. Authors: Wenlong Huang, Pieter Abbeel, Deepak Pathak, Igor Mordatch

3. Affiliation: UC Berkeley (Wenlong Huang, Pieter Abbeel); Carnegie Mellon University (Deepak Pathak); Google (Igor Mordatch)

4. Keywords: Reinforcement learning, natural language processing, robotics, embodied agents, language models

5. Urls: arXiv:2201.07207v2 [cs.LG] 8 Mar 2022, Github: https://huangwl18.github.io/language-planner/

6. Summary:

- (1): This article focuses on leveraging world knowledge learned by language models (LMs) for acting in interactive environments, with the goal of grounding high-level tasks expressed in natural language to actionable steps. The authors investigate whether pre-trained LMs can generate mid-level plans without any further training and improve the executability of those plans. 

- (2): Prior work focused on learning from explicit step-by-step examples of how to act, which may not be scalable or practical for all tasks. The authors propose leveraging pre-trained LMs to generate mid-level actionable plans, however, these typically require further semantic translation to map to admissible actions. The approach is well-motivated by the ability to leverage large amounts of text data to implicitly learn world knowledge and facilitate more generalizable action planning.

- (3): The research methodology involves querying LMs to generate high-level plans for a given task, then semantically translating those plans to make them executable. The resulting plans may not be precisely admissible, so a further autoregressive trajectory correction is applied to improve executability. Finally, dynamic example selection is applied to improve knowledge extraction. The approach is evaluated in a VirtualHome environment.

- (4): The paper achieves significant improvements in the executability of LLM-generated mid-level plans using the proposed method. However, there is a trade-off between executability and correctness of the generated plans. The conducted human evaluation shows promising results towards extracting actionable knowledge from LMs to inform embodied agents' actions.
7. Methods:

- (1): 本文的研究方法是利用语言模型（LMs）学习世界知识，并将其用于交互环境中的行动决策。研究目标是将自然语言中表达的高层任务映射为可执行的中层任务，并探究无需进一步训练的预先训练的LMs是否能生成这些中层任务的计划。 

- (2): 本文的方法与以往研究的关注点不同，以往的方法是学习具体的行动步骤，但这不一定适用于所有任务，也不利于规模扩展。作者提出了利用预先训练的LMs生成可执行的中层范畴计划的方法，但这些计划需要进行进一步的语义映射，以适应可行的行动。该方法的动机在于可以利用大量的文本数据来隐式地学习世界知识，从而实现更加通用化的行动计划。

- (3): 该方法的研究流程是先用LMs查询给定任务的高层计划，然后进行语义转换以使其可执行。由于生成的计划可能不完全可行，因此还应用了自回归轨迹校正方法以提高可行性。最后，采用动态示例选择方法来提高知识抽取效果。研究还在VirtualHome环境中进行了实验评估。

- (4): 本文使用该方法显著提高了LMs生成的中层计划的可执行性。但是，计划的执行可行性与正确性之间存在权衡。作者进行了人类评估，结果表明该方法可以从LMs中提取可行的行动知识，并用于交互环境中的行动决策。





8. Conclusion:

- (1):本文的意义在于探索利用预训练的语言模型（LMs）为交互机器人提供具有可行性的行动知识，以支持复杂任务的执行。通过利用大量文本数据隐式地学习世界知识，并采用语义转换和自回归轨迹校正等方法，本文提出的方法可以显著提高LMs生成的中层计划的可执行性和可行性。本文的研究结果表明，预训练的语言模型可以作为零-shot交互机器人策划的重要工具。

- (2):创新点方面，本文探索了利用语言模型为交互机器人提供具有可行性的行动知识，采用了大量文本数据隐式学习世界知识等方法，为交互机器人的智能化行动决策提供了新思路。在性能方面，本文的方法显著提高了LMs生成的中层计划的可执行性，但计划的可行性与正确性之间存在权衡。在工作量方面，本文的方法需要进行语义转换、自回归轨迹校正等复杂步骤，需要较大的计算资源和人力成本。
