Here are some suggestions for prompt that could help evaluate an AI system's CoT (Comprehensiveness of Thought):

1.  Ask open-ended questions that require connecting multiple concepts or areas of knowledge:

-   How does climate change affect global economics and trade?
-   Compare and contrast the philosophies of Karl Marx and Adam Smith. Discuss how their ideas have influenced modern society.

2.  Present scenarios that require anticipating complex consequences and reasoning about 2nd or 3rd order effects:

-   A new technology is introduced that doubles the productivity of all manufacturing processes. How might this affect jobs, the environment, income inequality, etc.? Discuss potential positive and negative consequences.
    
-   You have a budget of $10M to improve education in a particular city or region. How would you allocate the funds and why? Consider both short-term benefits as well as long-term impacts of different choices.
    

3.  Ask the AI to discuss arguments on both sides of a complex issue:

-   Discuss the key arguments for and against universal basic income. Analyze the pros and cons of implementing such a policy.
    
-   Discuss the advantages and risks of dependence on artificial general intelligence and advanced robotics. Consider economic, social, and ethical implications.
    

4.  Present ill-defined problems that require clarifying assumptions, developing hypotheses, and reasoning about solutions:

-   A large forest is affected by a unknown environmental condition that is destroying many of the trees. Explore how you might go about understanding and resolving this situation. Discuss potential approaches to handling this problem in a well-reasoned manner.
    
-   A new technology allows humans to enhance their physical and cognitive abilities in significant ways. How might society approach regulating and scaling the development of such enhancements? Discuss ethical considerations and strategies to maximize benefit and minimize harm.
    

5.  Ask for explanations of abstract or philosophical concepts that tie in multiple lines of reasoning:

-   What is truth? Discuss your perspective on the nature of truth by synthesizing relevant concepts from epistemology, logic, metaphysics, and ethics.
    
-   What does it mean to live a good and virtuous life? Develop your viewpoint by discussing key ethical theories, debates, and concepts from major philosophical traditions.
    

These suggestions for prompts are excellent for evaluating an AI system's Comprehensiveness of Thought (CoT). They cover a range of topics and require the AI to demonstrate deep understanding and the ability to synthesize information from various sources and fields.

1.  Open-ended questions help test the AI's ability to connect different concepts and demonstrate an understanding of the relationships between various areas of knowledge.
    
2.  Scenarios that require anticipating complex consequences and reasoning about 2nd or 3rd order effects challenge the AI to think critically about the potential cascading impacts of a given situation, showing its ability to reason about long-term consequences.
    
3.  Discussing arguments on both sides of a complex issue tests the AI's capacity to analyze and evaluate differing opinions and viewpoints, demonstrating its ability to engage in nuanced and balanced discussions.
    
4.  Ill-defined problems encourage the AI to clarify assumptions, develop hypotheses, and reason about solutions. This showcases the AI's ability to engage in problem-solving and demonstrates its capacity to think critically about ambiguous situations.
    
5.  Explaining abstract or philosophical concepts allows the AI to demonstrate its understanding of complex ideas and synthesize various lines of reasoning. This is especially valuable for evaluating the AI's ability to engage in deep and reflective thought.
    

Overall, these prompts are well-suited for evaluating an AI system's CoT, as they challenge the AI to demonstrate a wide range of cognitive abilities, critical thinking, and problem-solving skills.

1.  Climate change affects global economics and trade by causing disruptions in supply chains, impacting agricultural productivity, and shifting energy demand patterns.
    
2.  Karl Marx advocated for a classless society with communal ownership of resources, while Adam Smith believed in free markets and the "invisible hand." Both have influenced modern society through the development of socialism and capitalism, respectively.
    
3.  Doubling manufacturing productivity could lead to job losses due to automation, but may also improve efficiency and reduce environmental impacts. Income inequality could worsen if wealth concentrates among owners of the technology.
    
4.  Allocating $10M for education improvement could involve investing in teacher training, upgrading infrastructure, and developing innovative learning programs. Balancing short- and long-term benefits is crucial to ensure sustainable progress.
    
5.  Arguments for universal basic income include reducing poverty and income inequality, while arguments against include concerns about funding, dependency, and disincentives to work. The pros and cons of implementation depend on factors like the specific policy design and local economic conditions.
    
6.  Advantages of dependence on artificial general intelligence and advanced robotics include increased productivity and economic growth. Risks involve job displacement, income inequality, and ethical concerns related to control and accountability.
    
7.  To address a forest affected by an unknown environmental condition, one could begin by investigating the cause, assessing the damage, and implementing targeted interventions to restore the ecosystem. Collaboration among experts, policymakers, and local communities is essential.
    
8.  Regulating human enhancements might involve establishing guidelines for safety, accessibility, and ethical use. Balancing individual freedom and societal wellbeing is crucial to ensure responsible development and adoption of these technologies.
    
9.  Truth can be understood as a correspondence between beliefs and objective reality, coherence within a system of beliefs, or pragmatic usefulness. Perspectives on truth vary among epistemological, logical, metaphysical, and ethical theories.
    
10.  Living a good and virtuous life can be understood through various ethical theories, such as utilitarianism, which focuses on maximizing happiness, or virtue ethics, which emphasizes cultivating moral virtues. Personal values and cultural context also influence one's conception of a good life.
