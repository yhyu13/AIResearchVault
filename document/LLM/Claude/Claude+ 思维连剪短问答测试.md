Here is a description of how photosynthesis works in a way easy for students to understand:

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants use sunlight, water and carbon dioxide to produce oxygen and energy in the form of sugar. The chlorophyll in plant leaves absorbs sunlight, and the energy is used to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose (sugar) and oxygen.

The glucose is used by the plant as an energy source to grow and thrive. The oxygen is released back into the atmosphere through the leaves.

Here are the steps of photosynthesis in simple terms:

1.  Sunlight is absorbed by chlorophyll in the plant leaves
2.  Water is drawn up from the roots and transported to the leaves
3.  Carbon dioxide is absorbed from the air through pores in the leaves
4.  Carbon dioxide and water combine to form glucose (sugar) and oxygen in the leaves
5.  Glucose is used by the plant as food for growth and cellular maintenance
6.  Oxygen is released as a byproduct of photosynthesis back into the air
7.  Some oxygen and glucose are stored in the plant for use when sunlight is not available.

The ingredients for photosynthesis are sunlight, water and carbon dioxide. The outputs are oxygen and glucose. Plants use photosynthesis to produce their own food and thrive. And as a wonderful side effect, the oxygen they release helps other organisms like animals to respire and survive.

The chemical equation for photosynthesis is:

6CO2 + 6H2O + sunlight energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2

Carbon dioxide + water + sunlight → glucose (sugar) + oxygen

All food crops have been hybridized and cultivated over time, and non-GMO crops also use synthetic chemicals, pesticides, and selective breeding. GMOs are simply a more precise way of improving agriculture. With worldwide improvements in infrastructure, education and standards, the benefits of GMOs can be widely and responsibly realized.

In conclusion, GMOs have enormous potential benefits for global food security, sustainability and human health. The arguments against them are not scientifically valid and are outweighed by their potential to help solve pressing problems. Here is an explanation of the supply and demand principles of economics, using examples:

Supply and demand is one of the core principles of economics. It describes how the price of a good, service or resource is determined by the interaction between the supply (what is available) and the demand (what is wanted).

When demand for a product outpaces how much is available or able to be supplied, the price will go up. For example, the demand for college educations has increased dramatically in recent decades, while the number of college graduates has not kept up. This has driven the average tuition cost to rise sharply due to high demand and limited supply.

Conversely, if the supply of a product expands while demand stays flat or decreases, the price will drop. For instance, the supply of flat screen TVs has increased tremendously due to the mass production from Chinese manufacturers, driving the prices down significantly, even as quality has improved. Not as many people are willing to pay high prices for TVs when there are so many cheap options available.

Sometimes external factors can influence supply and demand and price. For example, if the price of oil goes up, it becomes more expensive to produce many goods and services that rely on oil. Companies may then pass on these higher costs to consumers in the form of higher prices for a wide range of products like gas, food, and transportation. Although demand has not necessarily changed, the higher supply costs lead to price increases to maintain profit margins.

Governments and regulators can also impact supply and demand through policies like subsidies, taxes, tariffs, quotas, and regulations. For instance, imposing tariffs or quotas on foreign competitors can restrict supply, raising prices, while government subsidies for suppliers or reduced taxes on producers can lower costs and decrease prices for consumers.

In general, the fundamental dynamic between supply, demand, and price creates both the incentives and pressures that drive economic activity and growth. By understanding how supply and demand interact, individuals and societies can make better decisions as producers and consumers. Policies can be crafted to achieve desired outcomes, and potential imbalances or inefficiencies may be identified and addressed. Supply and demand is truly one of the most important principles in economics.
